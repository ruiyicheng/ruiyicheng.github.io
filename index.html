About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Astronomy at the Tsung-Dao Lee Institute, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, advised by Prof. Fabo Feng. I expect to complete my PhD in June 2027. My research connects exoplanet detection and time-domain astronomy with AI for science and scientific evaluation of AI.

Large astronomical surveys produce far more observations than researchers can inspect individually. I develop tools that turn these data into reliable measurements and interpretable scientific evidence, from observation control and distributed data processing for the Tianyu telescope to high-precision photometry, astrometry, and realistic sky-image simulation.

Building on this work, I study representation learning for irregular light curves and develop AI agents that combine catalog information, period searches, and visual diagnostics to assess variable stars. I also contribute astronomy tasks and reviews to Terminal-Bench Science to evaluate how AI agents handle scientific analysis.

Outside research, I enjoy classical music, history, astrophotography, Dota 2, and robotics.

Research Projects

Tianyu Project (Full-stack Developer)
Jun 2023 - Present

Tianyu is a 1-meter robotic survey telescope in Lenghu, built to study exoplanets, variable stars, and transients.

  • Develop observation-control, scheduling, and survey-analysis software.
  • Built a distributed MySQL + RabbitMQ pipeline for image calibration, alignment, photometry, and light-curve production.
  • Developed relative photometry approaching the theoretical noise limit on real telescope data, and estimated exoplanet and transient yields.

Project · Paper

Light-Curve Representation Learning with JEPA
Nov 2025 - Present

Irregular sampling and measurement noise make astronomical light curves challenging for general-purpose time-series models.

  • Developed domain-informed multi-view self-distillation using raw light curves, periodograms, and phase-folded views.
  • Learned transferable representations for classification, similarity search, parameter estimation, and photometric zero-point drift detection.
  • Evaluated cross-domain adaptation to other irregular time-series datasets.

Preprint

Scientific AI Agents and Evaluation
Mar 2026 - Present

Variable-star vetting requires combining numerical tests, diagnostic plots, and source context into a traceable assessment.

  • Developing a vision-language-model agent that combines Gaia, LAMOST, and VSX context with period searches, phase folding, and iterative visual diagnostics.
  • Produces evidence-based reports with classifications, uncertainties, and follow-up priorities.
  • Contributed two accepted Terminal-Bench Science v0.1 tasks: variable-star vetting and joint radial-velocity/astrometry inference; also served as a reviewer.

Code · Vetting task · Orbit task

LenghuSky-8: Cloud Segmentation and Weather Nowcasting
Oct 2025 - Feb 2026

Reliable cloud monitoring supports astronomical site characterization and autonomous telescope scheduling.

  • Built an eight-year dataset of approximately 429,000 all-sky images from Lenghu, Qinghai.
  • Performed absolute astrometric and altitude-azimuth calibration and constructed star-aware cloud masks.
  • Benchmarked segmentation and nowcasting models, including DINOv3 linear probing and ConvLSTM; published in CVPR 2026 Findings.

Code and data · Paper

AstroSkyFlow: Astronomical Image Simulation
Nov 2024 - Jun 2026

Rare astronomical events and complex observing conditions make realistic simulations essential for validating survey pipelines.

  • Developed a modular simulator combining variable celestial sources, atmospheric and instrument effects, and detector noise.
  • Generates time-series sky images for machine-learning datasets and injection-recovery tests.
  • Validated photometric and motion signals, including exoplanet transits and asteroid trails.

Paper

DeepAP: Fast Aperture Photometry
Nov 2024 - May 2025

Choosing suitable sources and apertures is a computational bottleneck in high-precision survey photometry.

  • Co-developed a two-stage framework using a Vision Transformer to assess photometry feasibility and a ResNet to predict aperture size.
  • Achieved approximately 59,000× speed-up over exhaustive aperture-size enumeration in the reported benchmark.
  • Released the source code and trained model for reproducible photometry workflows.

Code · Paper

Mitigating Astrometric Bias
Dec 2021 - Jun 2023

Errors in stellar astrometry can bias barycentric corrections and obscure the weak radial-velocity signals of Earth-like planets.

  • Developed a gradient-based method using PEXO to correct biases in stellar astrometric parameters.
  • Studied the impact of astrometric systematics on ultra-precise radial-velocity measurements.
